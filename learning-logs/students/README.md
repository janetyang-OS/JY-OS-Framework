
# Student Learning Dataset

This directory contains pseudonymized student learning records used in the JY-OS Reading Judgment Lab.

The dataset documents longitudinal observations of:

- Reasoning development
- Execution discipline and learning consistency
- Language skill stabilization
- Evidence-based reading judgment
- AI-assisted coaching interactions
- Learning autonomy
- Execution continuity and interruption patterns
- Skill retention and transfer

The purpose is to identify reusable learning mechanisms and evaluate the conditions under which structured AI coaching supports observable academic development.

The dataset is observational. Coaching outcomes should not automatically be interpreted as causal proof of instructional effectiveness.

---

## 1. Research Architecture

The dataset follows one central governance principle:

**Student = Evidence Carrier**

**Track = System Asset**

Students provide longitudinal evidence.

Instructional tracks provide the reusable architecture for analysis, intervention design, and future Playbook development.

The research focuses on transferable learning patterns rather than individual success stories.

The Record ID column should match the actual repository directory.

Grade information and repository identifiers are separate metadata fields.

## 4. Student Status Definitions

### Active

The student is participating in regular weekly or biweekly training.

New assessments and learning signals may be added.

### Paused

Training is temporarily suspended.

During an approved pause:

- No formal task minimum applies.
- No academic progression is inferred.
- No performance score is assigned without new work.
- Missing submissions are not automatically classified as learning errors.

### Exited

The student is no longer participating.

Historical learning records remain available for analysis.

No future academic progress is assumed.

### Exited — Case Closed

A student has discontinued training and a final research case has been documented.

The case remains available for system-level analysis.

No further Weekly Signal Reports are generated.

### Intake Diagnostic

The student is undergoing initial assessment.

Baseline evidence is collected before a stable intervention protocol is established.

Weekly or biweekly assessment records.

These may include:

- Skill classifications
- Error patterns
- Intervention results
- Transfer verification
- Independent execution evidence

Initial assessment records and baseline gap analysis.

Historical records of interrupted or suspended training.

### case-library/

Extracted research cases based on meaningful longitudinal patterns.

Case studies should preserve the distinction between observed outcomes, interpretations, and unverified hypotheses.

Existing folder structures may vary by student.

Do not reorganize historical directories solely to standardize naming.

## 7. Case Study Extraction Rule

Weekly learning logs are not automatically research case studies.

A case study should be extracted only when a meaningful and sufficiently documented system pattern emerges.

Examples include:

- Sentence Reader to System Reader transition
- Evidence retrieval stabilization
- Signal versus perception mismatch
- Scaffold dependence and removal
- Independent reasoning transfer
- Output compliance stabilization
- Low-volume skill stability
- Execution continuity constraints
- Intervention-to-maintenance transition
- Learning-system fragmentation

A case study should document:

1. Baseline problem
2. Intervention
3. Observed evidence
4. Developmental change
5. Remaining bottleneck
6. System-level interpretation
7. Limitations
8. Transferable learning principle

Case studies must distinguish:

**Observed Evidence**

from:

**Coach Interpretation**

from:

**Research Hypothesis**

## 8. Research Evidence Standards

### Submitted Quality vs Verified Capability

A strong submitted answer does not automatically establish independent mastery.

Where relevant, assessment records should distinguish:

- First-submission quality
- Corrected final quality
- Independent execution
- Time-limit compliance
- Evidence completeness
- Transfer to unfamiliar tasks
- Actual school assessment results

### Intervention Exposure vs Skill Acquisition

Participation in a training activity does not necessarily prove the targeted skill has been acquired.

Original responses and assessment evidence should support progression claims.

### Missing Data vs Academic Regression

An absence of submissions during a pause or after exit must not be interpreted as declining academic ability.

### Coach Results vs Official School Results

AI Coach classifications are diagnostic research observations.

They are not official school-issued grades or standardized test outcomes.

## 9. Research Purpose

The JY-OS Reading Judgment Lab investigates how structured AI-supported learning protocols may help students develop:

- Evidence-based reading
- Controlled inference
- Passage-level reasoning
- Writing structure
- Language accuracy
- Independent judgment
- Revision discipline
- Skill stabilization
- Learning ownership

The system uses:

- Structured prompting
- Error-pattern tracking
- Evidence-retrieval protocols
- Targeted correction
- Redo systems
- Independent verification
- Longitudinal reporting

The research seeks to identify which learning mechanisms are repeatable, under what conditions they remain effective, and where implementation constraints limit the results.

## 10. Current Research Priorities

### Priority 1 — Structural Skill Stability

Determine whether previously corrected weaknesses remain stable across repeated independent tasks.

### Priority 2 — Independent Execution

Separate polished submissions from independently verified academic performance.

### Priority 3 — Error Pattern Transitions

Identify how the primary bottleneck changes as earlier weaknesses stabilize.

### Priority 4 — Sustainable Learning Dose

Observe how training frequency and workload affect the ability to maintain intervention continuity.

### Priority 5 — Cross-Case Pattern Extraction

Compare cases by learning mechanism rather than only by student age, grade, or outcome.

---

## 11. Governance Rules

### Rule 1 — Tracks Before Students

Every new learning record should identify the instructional track it contributes to.

### Rule 2 — Preserve Historical Evidence

Do not erase earlier errors, revisions, pauses, or unsuccessful interventions.

### Rule 3 — No Automatic Progression

Skill advancement requires relevant assessment evidence.

### Rule 4 — No Automatic Grade Promotion for Exited Students

Historical grade information remains fixed unless verified corrections are necessary.

### Rule 5 — No Unverified Causal Claims

Observed improvements should not be described as caused solely by AI coaching without sufficient supporting evidence.

### Rule 6 — Maintain Research Continuity

Avoid unnecessary folder renaming, duplicated cases, and fragmented record structures.

### Rule 7 — Protect Student Privacy

Exclude real names, school identifiers, family details, and other identifying information from public research cases.

---

## 12. Privacy Notice

Student aliases and coded identifiers are used to reduce direct identification risks.

These records should be treated as pseudonymized rather than fully anonymized because longitudinal information may still permit indirect identification.

Public-facing case studies should use independent case identifiers and minimize personal details.

Private student-level records and public research artifacts should remain logically separated.

---

## 13. Long-Term Playbook Objective

The dataset is intended to support future extraction of reusable educational infrastructure.

The long-term objective is to document:

- Standardized diagnostic procedures
- Evidence-based reasoning protocols
- Error classification methods
- Intervention and maintenance rules
- Independent verification standards
- Learning continuity constraints
- Repeatable case-study structures

The goal is not to accumulate student success stories.

The goal is to understand which learning mechanisms can be standardized, tested, and transferred across different students and educational contexts.
