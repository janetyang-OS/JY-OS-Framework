## Purpose

This file records Eddie’s initial diagnostic profile before full GSAT Reading OS training.

---

## Student Position

Eddie is a G10 private school student entering the GSAT Reading OS Diagnostic Track.

Initial hypothesis:

Eddie does not lack basic English foundation.

The main issue is that his reading system is still transitioning from:

→ Sentence Reader

to:

→ System Reader

This means he can often process individual sentences, but does not yet consistently build the full passage structure, evidence logic, and inference boundary required for GSAT-level academic reading.

---

## Core Diagnostic Judgment

Eddie’s gap is not simply:

* vocabulary
* grammar
* effort
* school English ability

The core gap is:

→ academic reading architecture

Specifically:

* sentence parsing under long or complex structures
* reading model identification
* inference boundary control
* evidence alignment
* confidence calibration
* background knowledge adaptation
* academic reading stamina

## Diagnostic Profile

### 1. Vocabulary Precision

Status:

→ Developing

Observation:

Eddie has a functional vocabulary base, but vocabulary precision is not yet stable enough for higher-level GSAT reading.

Main risk:

He may recognize a word generally, but miss:

* contextual meaning
* academic usage
* contrastive meaning
* implied tone
* word-form precision

Training implication:

Vocabulary should not be trained as isolated word memorization.

It should be trained through:

* context inference
* sentence function
* paragraph logic
* passage model

---

### 2. Sentence Parsing

Status:

→ High-priority gap at intake

Observed risk:

Long sentences may create instability when they contain:

* relative clauses
* participial phrases
* modifiers
* inserted phrases
* complex noun phrases
* multiple verbs

Initial behavior pattern:

Eddie may default to reading sentence by sentence or translating too much detail instead of extracting core meaning.

Required control:

For every difficult sentence, Eddie must identify:

1. Main Subject
2. Main Verb
3. Modifier
4. Relative Clause
5. Participial Phrase
6. Core Meaning

Training target:

Move from:

→ word-by-word translation

to:

→ structure-based sentence control

---

### 3. Reading Model Building

Status:

→ Largest diagnostic gap

Observed risk:

Eddie may understand individual sentences but fail to identify the passage’s operating structure.

This is the key difference between a sentence reader and a system reader.

Required models:

* Timeline
* Cause Chain
* Process
* Argument
* Problem–Solution

Training target:

Before answering questions, Eddie must identify:

1. What model is this passage using?
2. What evidence shows this model?
3. Why is it not another model?
4. How does the model help answer inference questions?

Common predicted errors:

* Process mistaken as Argument
* Process mistaken as Cause Chain
* Argument mistaken as Problem–Solution
* Detail sequence mistaken as Timeline

---

### 4. Evidence Control

Status:

→ Developing

Observed risk:

Eddie may find relevant text but not always align evidence with the question type.

Main issue:

Evidence may be:

* too broad
* too close to surface wording
* not directly connected to the answer
* repeated as the answer without reasoning

Required rule:

No answer should be confirmed without evidence.

Evidence must support the specific question being answered.

---

### 5. Inference Boundary Control

Status:

→ High-priority gap at intake

Observed risk:

Eddie may rely too heavily on directly located sentences.

Inference answers may become:

* repeated evidence
* unsupported extension
* personal opinion
* personality judgment
* over-generalized conclusion

Required format:

Evidence:
Reasoning:
Answer:
Confidence:

Training target:

Move from:

→ finding a sentence

to:

→ explaining how evidence leads to an answer

Important rule:

Inference must stay inside the article boundary.

No outside assumptions unless the question explicitly requires background knowledge.

---

### 6. Background Knowledge Reading

Status:

→ Diagnostic risk

Observed risk:

Unfamiliar topics may reduce reading stability.

Potential risk areas:

* history
* science
* geography
* psychology
* culture
* technology
* social issues

Training implication:

Eddie should not only read familiar school-English topics.

He needs controlled exposure to varied academic passages.

Recommended weekly passage distribution:

* History × 1
* Science × 1
* Geography × 1
* Psychology / Human Behavior × 1
* Society / Culture × 1

Purpose:

Build GSAT-style unfamiliar-topic tolerance.

---

### 7. Confidence Calibration

Status:

→ Developing

Observed risk:

Eddie may underestimate correct reasoning when confidence feels low.

Possible pattern:

* correct evidence
* reasonable answer
* low confidence rating

This matters because GSAT reading requires decision-making under uncertainty.

Training rule:

Confidence should be based on evidence quality, not on emotional certainty.

Required reflection when confidence ≤ 2:

Why is your confidence low?
Is the evidence weak, or are you only uncertain because the topic feels unfamiliar?

---

### 8. Task Timing

Status:

→ New risk to observe

Observed risk:

Late-night task completion may affect long-term stability.

Potential impact:

* reduced reading stamina
* weaker inference precision
* careless evidence alignment
* unstable execution consistency

Tracking required:

* task start time
* task completion time
* submission time
* reason for late submission if after midnight

Important distinction:

Late task completion may reflect:

* scheduling constraint
* procrastination
* night-owl study habit
* workload overload

Do not classify prematurely.

Observe for 2–3 weeks.

## Initial Error Code Set

### E-MODEL-WRONG

Reading model selected incorrectly.

Examples:

* Process selected when passage is Argument
* Cause Chain selected when passage is Problem–Solution
* Timeline selected when passage is only a sequence of examples

---

### E-INFER-JUMP

Inference moves beyond the article boundary.

Examples:

* unsupported interpretation
* personality judgment
* personal opinion inserted
* conclusion stronger than evidence allows

---

### E-EVID-MISALIGN

Evidence is present but does not support the answer precisely.

Examples:

* evidence answers a different question
* evidence is too general
* evidence is repeated without reasoning
* evidence does not match inference target

---

### E-SENT-PARSE-DRIFT

Long sentence structure becomes unstable.

Examples:

* main subject missed
* main verb misidentified
* relative clause mistaken as main idea
* participial phrase misread as main action

---

### E-CONFIDENCE-MISCALIBRATION

Confidence rating does not match evidence quality.

Examples:

* correct answer with strong evidence but confidence 1/5
* weak evidence but confidence too high
* uncertainty based on feeling rather than evidence

## Training Architecture

### Phase 1 — Sentence Parsing Stabilization

Goal:

Reduce long-sentence instability.

Tools:

* Main Subject
* Main Verb
* Modifier
* Relative Clause
* Participial Phrase
* Core Meaning

---

### Phase 2 — Reading Model Building

Goal:

Identify passage structure before answering questions.

Models:

* Timeline
* Cause Chain
* Process
* Argument
* Problem–Solution

Required format:

Evidence for Model:
Why not another model:
Final Model:

---

### Phase 3 — Inference Chain Control

Goal:

Make inference visible and bounded.

Required format:

Evidence:
Reasoning:
Answer:
Confidence:

Rule:

Student may not repeat evidence as the answer.

Reasoning must contain at least one logical connection.

---

### Phase 4 — Academic Reading Adaptation

Goal:

Build tolerance for unfamiliar GSAT-style passages.

Topics:

* History
* Science
* Geography
* Psychology / Human Behavior
* Society / Culture

---

### Phase 5 — Confidence Calibration

Goal:

Align confidence rating with evidence quality.

Rule:

If confidence is low but evidence is strong, student must explain the mismatch.

---

## Coach Execution Rules

Do not rewrite student responses.

Do not provide model answers.

Do not reveal answer keys.

Do not provide Relevant Passage hints before questions.

Require evidence before correctness confirmation.

Require model identification before passage discussion.

Student must retrieve evidence independently.

If confidence ≤ 2 but the answer is supported, require explanation of confidence rating.
