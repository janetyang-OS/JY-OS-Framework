# Reading Judgment Lab

The **Reading Judgment Lab** documents small learning experiments conducted in an AI-assisted learning environment.

The goal of this lab is to study a simple but critical question:

**How can students make their thinking visible?**

In many modern education systems, students may understand a concept but struggle to explain their reasoning clearly.  
This often creates a gap between **conceptual understanding** and **communicated reasoning**.

This lab records observations from real learning sessions where AI tools are used not as answer generators, but as **reasoning partners**.

---

## Research Focus

The experiments in this lab focus on three questions:

1. How students structure reasoning when solving problems
2. How AI can support reasoning visibility
3. How small protocol changes affect learning outcomes

The work is inspired by real classroom frameworks such as IB and CEFR, where communication of reasoning is an essential part of assessment.

---

## Lab Structure

This repository documents three types of artifacts.

### Reasoning Models

Conceptual frameworks used to help students structure thinking.

Example:


This model encourages students to explain:

• **WHY** the method is used  
• **WHAT** rule or relationship applies  
• **HOW** the steps connect logically

---

### Protocols

Repeatable learning protocols used during AI-assisted sessions.

Examples include:

• AI reasoning prompts  
• short intervention sessions  
• explanation scaffolding

---

### Case Library

Documented learning observations from real students.

Each case includes:

• initial problem signal  
• intervention protocol  
• observed outcome

These case records serve as small learning experiments rather than formal research studies.

---

## Purpose

This lab is part of a broader effort to explore how AI can support **reasoning development rather than answer production**.

The long-term goal is to build an **evidence-based playbook for AI-assisted learning**.
