# Case 001 — Logan: The Mathematical Reasoning Gap

## Context

Student: Logan  
Program: IB MYP (Year 9)  
Subject: Mathematics

During a routine assessment review, an unusual score pattern appeared:

| Criterion | Score |
|-----------|------|
| Criterion A (Knowing & Understanding) | 6 |
| Criterion C/D (Communication & Application) | 2–3 |

This pattern suggested that the student **understood the mathematical concept but could not clearly communicate the reasoning process**.

In the IB framework, this gap usually indicates that reasoning exists internally but is **not visible in written explanations**.

---

## Initial Signal

Observation during problem solving:

• Logan often produced the correct answer quickly  
• However, explanations skipped key reasoning steps  
• Intermediate logical links were missing  
• Mathematical rules were applied but not explicitly stated

This created a situation where the final answer was correct, but the **reasoning chain was incomplete**.

---

## Intervention

A short intervention was conducted using an AI math coach.

Duration:
3 sessions

Schedule:

Friday  
Saturday  
Sunday

Session length:

< 30 minutes per session

The AI coach did **not generate answers**.

Instead, the AI repeatedly prompted the student to structure explanations using a simple reasoning framework.

---

## Reasoning Model Used

The explanation structure introduced was:


Students were prompted to explain:

**WHY**  
Why this mathematical method is used.

**WHAT**  
What rule, formula, or relationship is applied.

**HOW**  
How the steps connect logically to reach the final result.

This model forces reasoning to become **explicit rather than assumed**.

---

## Observed Change

After three short sessions:

| Criterion | Before | After |
|-----------|-------|------|
| Criterion A | 6 | 6 |
| Criterion C/D | 2–3 | 6 |

The improvement did **not come from learning new mathematics**.

Instead, the improvement came from making the reasoning structure visible.

The student began to:

• state the rule being applied  
• connect intermediate steps  
• justify transitions between steps

---

## Key Observation

AI was most effective when used as a **reasoning partner rather than a solution generator**.

Instead of providing answers, the AI continuously asked the student to clarify reasoning.

This small shift helped the student externalize thinking that previously remained implicit.

---

## Notes

This case represents a small learning observation rather than a formal experiment.

However, it illustrates a common pattern:

Students may possess conceptual understanding but lack the structured language needed to communicate reasoning clearly.

Simple explanation frameworks, combined with short AI-assisted sessions, may help close this gap.
